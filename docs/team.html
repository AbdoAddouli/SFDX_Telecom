One plan, four people

80 project duties + 12 recurring duties · 11 phases · zero unassigned work

This page takes every duty described in the documentation set and assigns it to a delivery team of exactly four people. Each duty keeps the skill domain implied by its original owner in use-cases/, and the allocation is balanced by effort so that no one is a bottleneck on paper. Nothing here changes the scope — it only decides who carries it. Source: use-cases/EPIC-00…EPIC-10, ARCHITECTURE.md, BUSINESS_PROCESS.md

Effort balance across the four — points, not calendar time

A duty is worth 5 points when it is Must, 3 when Should, 1 otherwise. The target line is the team average — a bar that reaches the dashed line is carrying its fair share.

Blocked work — and who owns the chase — README §8 decision register

⚠️
Blocked duties are assigned, but they cannot start. Chasing the open decisions is itself a duty (see Responsibility) and is deliberately held by one accountable person, not shared.
Why four?
The documentation names more than a dozen roles (architect, Apex developer, admin, pricing admin, LWC developer, Experience Cloud developer, DevOps, QA, security, ops, analyst). A team of four means each person covers a set of domains — that is the trade-off this page makes visible.
How the split was chosen
Each duty carries a demand domain from its original owner label. A duty goes to the least-loaded person whose domains cover it, so Apex work never lands on the portal developer and pricing work never lands on the CI/CD owner.
What to watch
Two things cause real trouble in a four-person team: single-point domains (only one person can do the work) and accountability stacking (one person signs everything off). Both are measured below.

Domain coverage & key-person risk — who can even do the work

A domain with a single capable person is a single point of failure: holidays, illness or a resignation stops that workstream. The last columns show the mitigation to plan for.

Recurring operational duties — the work that never appears in a backlog

These are not project use cases: they are the standing duties the documentation implies (pricing export refresh, reconciliation triage, runbook upkeep, gate evidence). They repeat whether or not the project is on track, so they are counted in hours per week and shown separately from effort points.
